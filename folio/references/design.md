# Editorial design workflow

Okou adaptation of Folio's design principles; these are composition choices, not
saved user preferences or permission to reproduce a reference.

## Make the printed object coherent

- **Narrative:** promise → explanation → evidence → next step. Put the strongest
  opening where a reader first meets the object; make the close useful, not a filler.
- **Hierarchy:** one dominant move per page/spread. Contrast display, lead, body and
  furniture clearly. Avoid pages made entirely of medium-sized headings and cards.
- **Rhythm:** alternate dense/quiet and light/dark deliberately. A cover, services
  spread and people page should not be the same grid with different words.
- **Spreads:** read even-left / odd-right. Align the shared band, rule or image across
  the gutter; keep critical faces, numbers and copy away from the binding.
- **Typography:** use real families/weights, optical spacing and readable measures.
  Do not force copy into fixed boxes by reducing everything to tiny type.
- **Imagery:** decide whether it proves a fact, establishes a setting or illustrates
  an idea. Give it a job and a crop. Empty photo slots are not finished design.
- **Colour:** retain deliberate paper/ink contrast and one or two accent roles.
  An intentional monochrome treatment is valid; a detector is not the art director.
- **Identity:** repeat a small set of meaningful motifs and consistent furniture.
  Do not confuse many ornaments, shadows or badges with sophistication.

## Build from the catalogue

Read the chosen KIT.md, then at most two or three relevant pattern cards from
library/INDEX.md. Reuse abstract grammar, not someone else's private study artwork.
Adapt page count and copy lengths explicitly. For a single flyer use one .page.single
canvas and a clear title–proof–action hierarchy; the book kits are not imposition files.

## Review from pixels before reading the detector

Inspect cover, every inside page, all spreads and the close. Ask:

1. Is the intended message obvious in three seconds?
2. Does each spread have an anchor, and does the sequence have rhythm?
3. Do type baselines, frame edges, column widths and running furniture align?
4. Is body copy readable at actual finished size, including CJK punctuation?
5. Are the pictures appropriate, sharp enough, correctly cropped and truthfully labeled?
6. Are all claims supported, placeholders replaced and required facts intact?
7. Is the result recognizably tailored to this content, not a colour-swapped sample?

List concrete fixes by page: P0 factual/production breakage; P1 hierarchy/readability;
P2 polish. Never equate a clean preflight, a similarity score or source-code inspection
with finished visual quality.
