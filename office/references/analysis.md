# Develop and check the analysis

Use for Office work that must explain, compare, recommend or establish
feasibility. Keep the user's sources, audience, scope and requested length.
The work below does not prescribe the document's headings or extra deliverables.

## Establish what the reader needs to decide or understand

Identify the question the document must answer and the evidence needed to
answer it. Read the relevant source content and calculate the requested results
before drafting findings around a document template. Source extraction and
environment checks can start immediately; file construction may reveal further
analytical questions to resolve.

For substantial analysis, keep the important workings in the task workspace:
source locations, executed calculations, material unresolved questions and the
judgments they support. Reuse a calculation script, notebook or existing notes;
do not create a second ledger or impose a fixed form. Retain material tool
results so the final figures can be checked. These are internal working materials,
not attachments to deliver unless requested.

## Turn findings into the next piece of work

After the initial results, look for the unresolved issue that could most change
the main explanation, recommendation or feasibility judgment. Choose the
applicable operation below, perform it, and use the result in the judgment.
Reassess when new evidence appears; do not run every operation on every task.

| Finding in the material or initial result | Work to perform when relevant |
| --- | --- |
| A total changed, but its explanation only repeats that change | Reconcile the change to meaningful components, offsets or segments. Where data support it, separate scale, rate and mix using a stated baseline. |
| A cause is proposed | Check the mechanism and evidence linking it to the outcome. Examine a plausible competing explanation; identify the observation that would distinguish them. |
| A proposal reaches a target | Recompute resources from the proposed activities and assumptions: fixed and variable costs, capacities, dependencies and deadlines. Check the binding constraint. |
| An option is recommended | Compare the credible alternative that could change the choice using consistent assumptions. Explain the benefit or coverage forgone, costs, risks and conditions of the recommendation. |
| A result depends on an uncertain input | Calculate a relevant threshold or sensitivity if available inputs permit it. State the condition under which the judgment changes. |
| Two source passages or numbers seem inconsistent | Check the identities and settings of the compared results in the original context before treating them as a contradiction. |

A missing cause does not make a measurable difference unquantifiable. Separate
work that can be completed from the specific judgment missing evidence prevents.
Complete the supported work, then narrow the unresolved question. Do not treat
listing a gap or proposing future analysis as completion of work possible now.

For example, if actual sales are below plan and cost is also below plan, the
absolute cost saving does not establish better cost performance. If a common
cost-to-sales basis is appropriate, calculate expected cost at actual sales
using the planned rate. The remaining difference is a rate/mix residual, not
proof of a particular supplier, productivity or pricing cause. Do not call it a
volume effect when quantities are unavailable, or assume proportional costs
when the cost structure does not support that basis.

Use available specialist guidance for a method you need: `flux-analysis` for
financial variance decomposition, `analysis-qa` for numerical-analysis checks,
or `business-skill-index` for a task-matched business or literature-review
reference. User interviews and scientific-paper comparisons need different
methods. These are optional resources, not installation requirements. A method
reference does not authorize new factual sources, extra scope or external actions.

## Check the claims that carry the conclusion

Prioritize the quantities, mechanisms, source interpretations and assumptions
whose failure would change the answer. For each, inspect the supporting passage,
record or calculation; a citation's existence does not establish support.

- For reported results, retain the entity/method, configuration, population,
  period, units, metric and split where they affect the comparison. Inspect the
  table headings, notes and surrounding text needed to resolve the identity.
  Different configurations can legitimately report different values.
- Before claiming a source is wrong, verify that the apparent conflict concerns
  the same result and check the original context. Preserve source-specific
  values and uncertainty when the permitted evidence cannot resolve it.
- For a calculation, execute the arithmetic using suitable tools and reconcile
  material totals to source inputs through an independent calculation or control.
  Check signs, denominators, types, missing values and actual source coverage.
- For an explanation, distinguish an accounting contribution, association,
  source-reported explanation and established cause. An arithmetic residual
  does not identify its business cause.

Use only the factual sources authorized by the user. When sources are bounded,
do the reasoning and arithmetic they support without adding external facts.
If an essential input is missing, ask only when clarification is permitted;
otherwise deliver the supported portion and qualify the affected conclusion.

## Rebuild proposed changes from their assumptions

Specify what changes and what stays constant in a scenario, including the basis
of any extrapolation. Recompute dependent results and test the affected resource
constraints. A proposal or illustration needs valid arithmetic and internally
consistent assumptions even when its outcome is uncertain.

For example, a campaign has a one-time setup fee plus channel spend. Increasing
the target with the same channel mix and unit costs requires recalculating
channel spend and adding the unchanged setup fee. Scaling all-in cost per result
also scales that fee; justify the added cost or use the component calculation.
If channel capacity or unit economics are unknown, the component calculation is
a conditional scenario, not a guaranteed outcome or a proven minimum budget.

Check the revised proposal as a whole. A change that fixes budget can create a
staffing conflict; a new learning assessment can consume training capacity; an
expedited order can change arrival timing without changing normal order rules.
Include the resource use of the recommended actions, not only the original plan.
Compare alternatives only as far as the requested choice requires. Do not
invent benefits, prices or causal effects to force a ranking.

## Accept the findings, then check the saved document

Before accepting the main answer, inspect the remaining material questions:

- Complete a calculation, comparison or source check when permitted evidence
  can resolve a question that would change the conclusion.
- If the evidence cannot resolve it, change the affected recommendation,
  confidence or conditions. Keep the qualification beside the claim it limits.
- Check whether a credible alternative or realistic input change would reverse
  the recommendation; include the relevant trade-off or threshold when supported.

Finish when the reader can use the answer without completing an essential
analysis left implicit, and unresolved evidence appropriately limits the result.
Do not add background, scenarios or sections solely to make the report longer.

Use the format-specific production and validation instructions. On the exact
saved deliverable, reconcile the main results, assumptions and recommendations
to the checked workings. Read the surrounding prose as well as tables and charts:
rounding, copied explanations or dropped qualifications can change the meaning.
When a result changes, update dependent statements and proposed actions.

Record which important checks actually ran and any that remain unavailable in
the existing working notes or tool log. Do not infer a content pass from a
package validator, a formula-error scan, a successful render or an intended test.
Keep internal skill paths, review notes and execution details out of the reader's
document unless they are needed to use it or the user requests them.
