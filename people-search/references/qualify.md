# Qualification Guide

## Turn the request into verifiable criteria

Start with the decision the user needs to make from the list. Pair each task requirement and decision-relevant implicit condition with a verifiable fact about the candidate. Preserve requested fields and distinguish hard requirements, ranking preferences, recent signals, and follow-up items. For each question, identify the subject, the relevant period or scope, and what evidence is sufficient.

Use the questions that apply to the task.

- **Identity and attribution.** Who does the evidence identify? When and in what context did the person hold the relevant role or responsibility, or receive the credit?
- **Relevant experience.** Which action, work, or result best demonstrates relevant experience? What did the person do themselves?
- **Match for this task.** What does the user need from the output, audience, setting, or working conditions? Which facts about the candidate show how well they match? Which decision would a gap affect?
- **Ability to move forward.** What conditions need to hold for the next step? Which recent signals could change when to follow up? What still needs confirmation?

Define the key Fit criteria, the time window for Intent, and the evidence required for each Intent level before applying them across batches. Recheck affected candidates whenever the task definition changes.

## Connect evidence to each judgment

For each key fact, record **the conclusion, the supporting fact, the original source, and where to find it**, such as a paragraph, credit, or video timestamp. Record event, publication, and verification dates separately. Use the original material returned by tools to decide which facts it supports. See [Tools and evidence](tool-evidence.md).

| Judgment | How to handle it |
| --- | --- |
| **Pass** | Evidence confirms that the person meets the requirement in the relevant scope and period. Count the candidate as eligible once all hard requirements pass. |
| **Ineligible** | Specific evidence shows that a hard requirement is unmet. Record the contradictory evidence. |
| **Unverified** | Key material is missing, attribution is uncertain, access is blocked, or sources conflict. Record what is established, what is missing, and where it could be verified. |

## Assess Fit and Intent

### Fit

Give each qualified candidate a single whole-number percentage from 0% to 100%. Define which preferences and evidence warrant 100% for this task, then use clearly defined lower bands to represent weaker matches. Use any weights specified by the user. Count each fact once when assessing Fit. Cite the work, responsibility, or other fact that best explains the score. When key profile details remain unknown, assign a provisional score based on the available evidence and explain what could change it.

For an English-language vertical tutorial, a candidate who passes eligibility but has an unverified format match might score 60%, one with similar English-language vertical tutorials might score 80%, and one with work matching the topic, format, and intended audience might score 100%. Set bands for the specific commission.

### Intent

Define the intended next action, whose intent matters, and the signal window. Cite who did what, when, and how it relates to this opportunity.

| Level or status | Evidence |
| --- | --- |
| **High** | Explicit, current interest or action directly relevant to the opportunity, such as a creator publicly inviting brand commissions of this kind. |
| **Medium** | Recent relevant activity or indirect evidence, such as a disclosure of similar recent work. Specific interest still needs confirmation. |
| **Low** | Clear evidence against interest, or review of the relevant sources yields only weak signals or none that meet the criteria. Record the contrary evidence or the sources checked. |
| **Unverified** | Relevant sources remain unchecked or inaccessible, or signals conflict. The evidence is insufficient to assign a level. |
| **Not applicable** | Intent is irrelevant to the task, such as identifying a speaker at a past event. |

Explain Fit and Intent separately and cite the evidence for each. Rank candidates according to the user's next action. If the goal is to find the strongest match, rank by Fit first and consider Intent among candidates with similar Fit. Determine eligibility separately against the hard requirements.

## When to enrich candidate profiles

Prioritize gaps that affect eligibility. If identity, current role, or attribution is unresolved, start with material that could settle the requirement. Once eligibility passes, investigate deeper Fit or follow-up details. For example, if an open-source account and its contributions are verified but the task requires a person's name, look for a professional profile or official project credit that explicitly connects the account and the name.

Reuse works, profiles, and release notes already reviewed. Once candidates can be compared, focus on facts that could change the ranking or the follow-up action. When sources repeat known facts and no new source is likely to answer the remaining question directly, record the facts and open question, then move to leads more likely to yield qualified candidates or improve the decision.

Record the platform and verification date for public account metrics such as follower or subscriber counts. Track the creator's location, the market their content targets, actual audience geography, and ability to receive a sample separately. Report geographic shares using analytics with a defined scope and date. Turn facts that public sources cannot confirm into specific follow-up questions, such as requesting the share of US viewers and the reporting period, or confirming whether the creator can receive a sample in the US.
