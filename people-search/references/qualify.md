# Qualification and Candidate Profiles

## Verify the user's basic requirements

Turn each hard requirement into a fact you can verify. Specify who or what it applies to, the relevant time period or scope, and what evidence is sufficient. Start with the requirement most likely to rule someone out. For key facts, record the original source and the relevant passage or timestamp. Distinguish event, publication, and verification dates. Use [Tools and evidence](tool-evidence.md) to judge what each source supports. A reliable source that directly states the fact for the right person and period establishes it; another source adds information only when the first is indirect, dated or disputed.

- **Qualified.** Evidence supports every hard requirement. Start building this person's task-specific profile as soon as they qualify.
- **Ineligible.** Evidence shows a hard requirement is unmet. Record why and move on to other people.
- **Unverified.** A required fact is missing or disputed. Look for a source that can resolve it directly. If none is available, keep the person as a lead, record the gap, and move on to other people.

## When to enrich candidate profiles

Start with the key facts you verified: who the person is, what they did themselves, and how that satisfies the user's requirements. Put those facts and their sources directly in the profile. Then research what the user still needs to know to choose a person or take the next step.

- **Derive the profile from the task.** If the user wants to know whether a creator can reach a target market, check their content language, stated audience, and any available geographic audience data. If the user needs someone who can lead a project, look for evidence of their own responsibilities, the project scope, and outcomes. Verify any of these facts during qualification when the user makes it a hard requirement.
- **Keep the profile usable.** Include requested fields and the facts most relevant to the decision. When the task depends on a creator's reach, record follower or subscriber counts and recent performance of relevant work by platform, with the date checked. Distinguish the creator's location, the market the content addresses, and the audience's actual geography. Use dated analytics with a defined scope to verify audience shares. Add public professional contact channels when needed.
- **Know when to stop.** Before looking for more, identify the missing fact, the decision it could change, and the source most likely to answer it. Stop once the profile supports the decision or the remaining sources are unlikely to close a material gap. If something needs confirmation from the person, state the specific question. Update eligibility if new evidence changes a required fact.
