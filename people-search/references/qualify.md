# Qualification Guide

Fit measures how well an eligible person matches this task. Use the initial Fit assessment to investigate and summarize the profile details that explain the match, help compare candidates, or support the next step. Assess Intent separately from recent evidence of willingness or action relevant to the opportunity.

## Turn the request into verifiable criteria

Start with the decision the user needs to make from the list. Pair each task requirement and decision-relevant implicit condition with a verifiable fact about the candidate. Preserve requested fields and distinguish hard requirements, ranking preferences, recent signals, and follow-up items. For each question, identify the subject, the relevant period or scope, and what evidence is sufficient.

Use the questions that apply to the task.

- **Identity and attribution.** Who does the evidence identify? When and in what context did the person hold the relevant role or responsibility, or receive the credit?
- **Relevant experience.** Which action, work, or result best demonstrates relevant experience? What did the person do themselves?
- **Match for this task.** What does the user need from the output, audience, setting, or working conditions? Which facts about the candidate show how well they match? Which decision would a gap affect?
- **Ability to move forward.** What conditions need to hold for the next step? Which recent signals could change when to follow up? What still needs confirmation?

Define the key Fit criteria, the time window for Intent, and the evidence required for each Intent level before applying them across batches. Recheck affected candidates whenever the task definition changes.

## Connect evidence to each judgment

For each key fact, record **the conclusion, the supporting fact, the original source, and where to find it**, such as a paragraph, credit, or video timestamp. Record event, publication, and verification dates separately. Use the original material returned by tools to decide which facts it supports. See [Tools and evidence](tool-evidence.md).

| Judgment | How to handle it |
| --- | --- |
| **Pass** | Evidence confirms that the person meets the requirement in the relevant scope and period. Count the candidate as eligible once all hard requirements pass. |
| **Ineligible** | Specific evidence shows that a hard requirement is unmet. Record the contradictory evidence. |
| **Unverified** | Key material is missing, attribution is uncertain, access is blocked, or sources conflict. Record what is established, what is missing, and where it could be verified. |

## Fit

1. **Build a rubric for the task.** Derive the Fit criteria from the user's decision and ranking preferences. For each criterion, specify what the task calls for, what to verify about the candidate, and what evidence would show a strong match, a partial match, or a clear mismatch. Set the criteria's importance according to their effect on the decision. Apply any weights the user specifies.
2. **Define overall score bands.** Before scoring, specify what combination of evidence warrants 100% and what verified matches and gaps each lower band represents. Apply the same rubric across batches. Count each fact once.
3. **Report a supported overall score.** Give each eligible candidate one whole-number percentage from 0% to 100%. Cite the matches and limitations that best explain the score. If evidence for a key criterion is missing, use a provisional score based on verified matches and explain what evidence could change it.

## When to enrich candidate profiles

Start from the initial Fit assessment. Identify the **evidence that explains the match, gaps that could change the ranking, and conditions that could affect the next step**. Reuse sources already reviewed. Look for facts that clarify the person's contribution, the context in which it applies, the degree of match, or practical limitations. Update Fit and its rationale if new evidence changes the assessment.

Summarize the findings in a profile the user can compare with others. Explain what the person did, what they were responsible for, how it relates to this task, and what still needs confirmation. Retain user-requested fields and add basic details that help comparison or follow-up. If the task depends on reach through a creator's account, record follower or subscriber counts, recent performance of relevant work, and the verification date by platform. Record the creator's location, intended market, actual audience geography, and delivery conditions separately. Use audience analytics with a defined scope and date for actual audience geography. Include contact channels and open questions where useful.

Resolve gaps that affect eligibility first. Once candidates can be compared, prioritize evidence that could change a recommendation or action. When sources repeat known facts and no new source can directly answer the remaining question, record what is known and what needs confirmation, then move to leads more likely to improve the result.

## Intent

Define **who would take which next step** and set a signal window appropriate to the opportunity. Record the actor, event date, specific statement or action, source, and relevance to the opportunity. Attribute organizational developments to the organization and individual statements or actions to the person.

| Level or status | Evidence |
| --- | --- |
| **High** | An explicit, current expression of willingness, or direct action showing that the party is pursuing this opportunity or a close match. |
| **Medium** | Recent relevant activity or a specific indirect signal that explains why follow-up is timely. Willingness still needs confirmation. |
| **Low** | A clear contrary signal, or only identifiable weak signals after reviewing relevant sources. State the evidence and the scope checked. |
| **Unverified** | Available evidence is insufficient to assign a level, including when relevant signals are absent from accessible sources, access is blocked, or evidence conflicts. |
| **Not applicable** | The user's decision does not depend on the other party's willingness or timing. |

Explain Fit and Intent separately and cite the evidence for each. Rank according to the user's decision. If the goal is to find the strongest match, rank by Fit first and use Intent to prioritize follow-up among candidates with similar Fit.
