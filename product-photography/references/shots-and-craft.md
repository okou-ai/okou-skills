# Shots and photographic craft

> Adapted and materially rewritten from SuperCMO photographic/mode guidance and
> buluslan scene composition. See [source notices](../THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md).

Choose a shot for its job, then a look for its treatment. Neither choice grants
permission to change a real product or make unsupported marketing claims.

## Shot selection

| Shot | Job and staging | Camera and focus | Guardrails |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `studio` | Isolate the product on a controlled background; default to neutral white when the user requests a white main image. Keep props out unless requested. | Show the complete outline at an evidenced angle, with enough depth of field for the visible product/printing. | Preserve true product color and a grounded contact shadow; reserve margins so no required component is cropped. Pure white is not transparency or marketplace certification. |
| `lifestyle` | Place the product in one plausible setting with a motivated time of day and light source. Limit props to those supporting the scene. | Let the product remain the sharpest, most legible subject; keep environmental context subordinate. | Do not add people in v1. Do not imply unverified use, consumption, waterproofing, heat resistance, or included accessories. |
| `macro` | Explain one observed material, joint, texture, or manufacturing detail. Use the reference area that actually shows it. | Specify the focus plane and scale cue; use a tight crop without inventing microscopic structure. | Request a better view when the source lacks the detail. A guessed close-up is not verified product photography. |
| `flat_lay` | Arrange the product on one surface, viewed from directly above. Use negative space as a compositional relationship, not an untextured void. | Keep the camera plane parallel to the surface, with consistent scale and readable component relationships. | Require an overhead reference if showing a new surface would invent unseen geometry; do not automatically lay an upright product on its side. |
| `hero` | Lead a brand campaign with one clear product focal point and restrained supporting art direction. | Choose a supported front/three-quarter angle; keep the product silhouette and identity clear at the delivery crop. | Reserve a copy region only when requested. Keep headlines, discounts, CTA layouts, and “best seller” badges out of this photography workflow. |

Resolve overlaps by the information the image must convey: material evidence →
`macro`; overhead arrangement → `flat_lay`; white isolated listing image →
`studio`; otherwise use the setting/campaign purpose to distinguish `lifestyle`
from `hero`. Clarify only when the distinction changes a consequential constraint.

## Independent look presets

| Preset | Physical treatment | Suitable adaptation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `neutral` | Neutral light, controlled highlights, low-distraction surroundings, faithful material/color response. | Start here for product documentation and clean studio frames. |
| `fresh_natural` | Daylight-like key, open midtones, a light supporting surface, two or three background/prop tones tied to objects. Keep shadows visibly grounded. | Citrus, mint, pale tile, or condensation are optional scene choices, not mandatory props or product claims. Add moisture only when appropriate and authorized for a real product. |
| `dark_luxury` | Low-key background with retained shadow detail, shaped edge separation, restrained highlights, and a tactile supporting surface. | Use stone, glass, metal, or a controlled haze only when the brief benefits; keep haze off identifying details. Dark does not mean crushed blacks or an unreadable label. |
| `custom` | Translate the user's visual direction into specific light, palette, surface, camera, and focus choices. | Do not enforce an imported category-style blacklist. Preserve the user's intended aesthetic unless it conflicts with accuracy or higher-priority rules. |

These are not registered Open Design styles. Do not pass their preset names as
`--style` values. Translate them into physical instructions in the raw prompt.

## Shared craft checks

- **Key light:** specify direction, apparent size/softness, approximate neutral or
  warm/cool character, contrast, and product/background separation. Describe
  off-camera lighting effects rather than accidentally placing a fixture in-frame.
- **Shadows:** include a contact shadow when the product rests on a surface.
  Align cast-shadow direction and edge softness with the key. Do not ask for a
  broad soft key and an unexplained razor-hard shadow from the same source.
- **Metal:** shape a controlled bright-to-dark reflection; avoid a uniform plastic
  sheen or clipped highlights that erase the rim/edges.
- **Glass/liquid:** use plausible edge light, refraction, transparency, and fill
  level. Do not hide the outline in a background of the same value.
- **Matte surfaces:** use gentle oblique light to show actual texture; do not add
  grain to a smooth product merely to make it look expensive.
- **Fabric:** preserve observed weave, drape, seam locations, and printed motifs;
  do not invent extra stitching or inflate thickness to imply higher quality.
- **Color:** bind each color to a specific object. Style may change the setting,
  not recolor the SKU. Strong colored spill must not obscure a reference color.
- **Optics:** name the desired perspective and focus behavior. An optional lens
  equivalent is an art-direction cue, never proof a camera/lens was used.
- **State:** keep caps, seals, hinges, contents, and component count as supplied.
  “Lifestyle” or “in use” alone does not authorize opening or disassembling.
- **Restraint:** replace vague “premium / 8K / award-winning” piles with visible
  lighting/material choices. Avoid arbitrary photographer names and camera-brand
  incantations; no fixed list is necessary to obtain a coherent look.
